Over the past few weeks, I have been speaking with people across Indian commercial insurance: underwriters, brokers, claims professionals, corporate buyers and operators.

I began with a familiar product instinct. Find the most repetitive work, understand the documents involved, and see where AI could make the process faster.

That framing was not wrong, but it was incomplete.

The pattern that keeps showing up is **information continuity**. The information required to make a good decision exists somewhere, but it rarely travels cleanly through the full workflow.

## A risk starts as a story and becomes a form

A company understands its own operations as a living system: what it makes, where it operates, what has changed, and what it is worried about. The insurer eventually receives structured fields, attachments, declarations and a broking slip.

Something is lost in that translation.

Missing data does not always mean someone is hiding information. A buyer may not know which detail is material. A broker may be coordinating across several parties. An underwriter may receive enough to start quoting but not enough to understand the risk with confidence.

The problem is not simply “collect more documents.” It is knowing which gaps matter, who can resolve them, and why the answer will affect the decision.

## The handoffs create their own work

Commercial placement is not one transaction. It is a chain of requests, clarifications, versions, quote terms and internal judgments.

The work grows when every participant has a different view of the current state:

- Which information has been confirmed?
- What is still an assumption?
- Why did one insurer add a condition that another did not?
- Which version of a document informed the quote?
- What was agreed verbally but never made explicit in the final record?

People compensate with calls, spreadsheets, email threads and memory. Those tools keep the process moving, but they do not preserve a reliable decision trail.

## Claims inherits the history

Claims sits downstream from all of this. By the time a loss occurs, the original placement decisions may be months or years old. The people involved may have changed. The reasoning behind an exception, warranty or coverage choice may be scattered across systems.

This makes claims look like a separate automation problem. In reality, part of the friction was created much earlier.

A faster claims interface cannot recover context that was never captured.

## What this changes about the product

I am becoming less interested in a system that tries to “do underwriting with AI” and more interested in one that makes the human workflow easier to see and trust.

That means a useful product should:

1. capture information once and preserve where it came from;
2. reveal material gaps before they become another email round;
3. keep quote differences tied to the assumptions behind them;
4. carry decision context forward into servicing and claims;
5. leave judgment with the people accountable for it.

This is a narrower promise than replacing an underwriter, broker or claims professional. It is also a more honest one.

The opportunity may not be a magical decision engine. It may be a shared layer that stops important context from disappearing every time the work changes hands.
