A job title is a weak map of who understands a workflow.

On September 30, I was arranging a conversation about commercial claims. The practitioner I had contacted said they might not be the right person for process focused questions, although they still offered a later catch-up. That response was useful before the conversation even happened. It marked a boundary around what this source could reliably tell me.

I have been researching insurance by approaching people across broking, underwriting, operations, and claims. Titles help me decide where to begin. They do not tell me which steps a person performs or which exceptions they see. They also rarely show how recently that person worked inside the process I am asking about.

That distinction matters because I am trying to understand work before deciding what Insuveo should become. A plausible title can produce a thoughtful conversation and still leave me with evidence from the wrong distance.

## Metadata gets me to the door

On October 1, an AI driven outreach workflow found six new India insurance prospects after screening suppression records, a master database, carry-forward records, and prior Gmail activity. The screening is valuable. It reduces duplicate outreach and keeps earlier work from disappearing each time the search runs.

The result is still a candidate list. It establishes that a person appears relevant from available records. It cannot establish that they personally handle the workflow I need to learn.

This resembles retrieval in an AI product. A system can find a document with the right terms and date. Those signals do not guarantee that the document answers the current question. Relevance also depends on why the source exists and what part of reality it records.

People have provenance too. A claims leader may understand policy and escalation patterns while seeing little of the daily evidence collection. Someone in operations may know the repeated handoffs but not own the final decision. Neither view is inferior. Problems begin when I treat them as interchangeable.

## Proximity changes the question

The right source depends on what I am trying to learn. A senior view may be necessary when I want to understand how a company prioritizes claims. Studying where information goes missing between a hospital, a claims team, and a customer requires someone who encounters that handoff often enough to remember its ordinary failures.

This changes the opening of a research conversation. I need to establish which part of the workflow the person touches before asking them to describe the whole system. When their view ends, that boundary should remain attached to the notes.

A referral can therefore be more valuable than a broad answer. A person who explains that the process sits outside their direct work prevents false confidence and may point toward someone closer to it. A research system should record that boundary as useful information.

## Retrieval should preserve perspective

Granveo began from a related concern: a conclusion loses value when its sources and changes disappear. Customer research adds another layer. The source may be credible and the account accurate within a limited position. If the synthesis removes that position, a local observation can become a market claim.

An AI agent supporting discovery should retain how directly a source observed a step. Any uncertainty should remain attached to the account. The agent should help compare different vantage points without smoothing them into one generic workflow. Producing an instant market summary may look impressive. Preserving perspective is what helps when I have to decide what to build.

Insuveo still has no confirmed design partner, and the current outreach has not validated a workflow. The immediate task is to improve the path from a plausible contact to a person with relevant operating knowledge. Some titles will lead directly there. Others will reveal that the map is wrong.

I want the retrieval system to learn from both outcomes. Finding the right person is partly a search problem. Knowing why that person is the right source requires a model of the work itself, and that model can only become accurate when people are allowed to describe the limits of what they know.
